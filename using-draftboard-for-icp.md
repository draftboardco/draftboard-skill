# Using Draftboard intros for your ICP

**Turn your Ideal Customer Profile from a definition into a warm-path action list.** Most tools use
your ICP to decide *who to chase*. With this skill, your ICP becomes the lens for *who you can
actually reach warmly right now* — so your best-fit accounts and your best-connected accounts stop
being two different lists.

You define (or discover) your ICP in Draftboard as usual; the people and companies that match get
grouped together. From there, you just ask — and the assistant works only inside that ideal-fit
group, finding the warm routes in.

---

## What you can do

> **When you ask "show me the warm intros into my ICP"**, you get a ranked shortlist of
> introductions — but only to people who fit your ideal profile. Each comes with who can introduce
> you and why they'll say yes. The win: your highest-intent accounts and your most-reachable
> accounts are finally the same view, so you work the overlap instead of choosing between fit and
> feasibility.

> **When you ask "of my ICP list, who am I already a step away from?"**, you get a triage: who
> you're directly connected to, who's one warm intro away, and who's genuinely cold. The win: you
> attack your ideal accounts in the right order — warm first — instead of treating a 5,000-name ICP
> as one undifferentiated wall.

> **When you hand it a set of ideal-fit people ("here are 30 perfect-fit prospects — track these as
> my ICP and tell me who I can reach")**, it adds them as a labeled segment and immediately reports
> your reachability across them. The win: a fresh target list becomes a prioritized outreach plan in
> one step.

> **When you ask "how's my ICP pipeline progressing?"**, you get a clean count — new, in motion,
> done — for just that segment. The win: you can report on your ideal-customer push without pulling
> a thing.

> **When you ask "within my ICP, who can my colleague Dana introduce me to?"**, you get the ideal-fit
> opportunities that run through a specific teammate's network. The win: you mobilize the whole
> team's relationships against your best accounts, not just your own.

Every answer tells you how much of the segment it actually examined and offers to go deeper — it
won't imply it swept your entire ICP when it sampled the top slice.

---

## The honest boundary

This skill is brilliant at **working the warm paths into an ICP you've already defined or
discovered**. It is *not* the thing that invents the list for you.

- **"Build my ICP target list from this written description of my ideal customer."** Generating
  fresh names from a text profile lives in the Draftboard app's discovery, not in the assistant yet.
  The flow that works today: define your ICP and let Draftboard surface matches in the app (they
  come back tagged as ideal-fit), *or* bring known good-fit names yourself — then the assistant takes
  it from there and routes the intros.

- **Where the "ideal-fit" grouping comes from.** You label an ideal-fit set yourself (add the people
  and tag them), or Draftboard's app groups a batch for you — either way the group is reachable by its
  tag **name**, and the assistant can aim at exactly that group. (Tags carry a `type` of `manual` or
  `automatic` only; there is no separate "ICP" tag type to filter on — target the group by its name.)

- **Freshly added people need a beat.** A just-imported prospect may not show a path immediately —
  Draftboard is still enriching them. "No path yet" can mean "not ready yet," and the assistant will
  say so rather than calling it cold.

---

## Start now (3 steps)

1. **Make sure your ICP is labeled in Draftboard** — either from the app's AI matching, or by adding
   a set of ideal-fit names and tagging them as your ICP segment.
2. **Connect the assistant** (one-time) — see the [setup guide](./draftboard-intros/references/setup.md).
3. **Ask**, naming your segment: *"Using Draftboard, show me my best warm intros into my ICP, and
   tell me which I'm already connected to."*

That's it — you've turned a profile into a prioritized, warm outreach plan.
